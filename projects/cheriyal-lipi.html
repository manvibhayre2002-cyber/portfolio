01

THE ART

Every scroll is a story told panel by panel.

Cheriyal scroll painting is a traditional folk art form from Telangana, India, known for its vibrant colours and storytelling style. Before drawing a single letter, I studied what makes a Cheriyal figure instantly recognisable.

“A letter can carry a story the way a Cheriyal figure does.” That became the brief for every glyph.

FORM

Bold outlines

Heavy, confident contours separate every figure from the flat painted ground.

FACE

Expressive eyes

Large almond eyes and stylised faces carry the emotion of each scene.

MOTIF

Animals, flora and vines

Ornament fills every gap, giving the scrolls their dense, maximal rhythm.

Cheriyal scroll painting comes from Cheriyal village in Telangana and is practised by the Nakashi family of artists.



The long painted scrolls, called patams, were made for travelling storytellers, who unrolled them panel by panel while singing myths, folk legends and caste histories.



More than a painting, each scroll is a story told in sequence, using colour, faces and everyday village life to hold a crowd's attention.

03

THE SCRIPTS

One visual language across four scripts.

The same Cheriyal vocabulary is carried into Telugu, Devanagari, Latin and Telugu numerals. The script changes; the storytelling holds.

CHERIYAL LIPI- TELUGU EDITION

typographic exploration blending Telugu script with the folk art of Cheriyal painting. Each letterform is infused with storytelling elements, traditional faces, and motifs. The result is a vibrant fusion of language and heritage through expressive design.

CHERIYAL LIPI- DEVNAGARI EDITION

blends Hindi letterforms with the expressive faces and motifs of Cheriyal folk painting. Each consonant becomes a narrative fragment, where script and storytelling merge seamlessly. The design celebrates India’s linguistic diversity through a unified visual heritage.

CHERIYAL LIPI- LATIN EDITION

fuses English alphabets with the rich visual storytelling of Cheriyal art. Each letterform becomes a cultural canvas, adorned with expressive faces and motifs rooted in Indian folk tradition. This cross-script exploration bridges global typography with local heritage.

REFLECTION

Heritage as a living type system.

Cheriyal Lipi bridges global typography with local heritage, celebrating India’s linguistic diversity through one shared visual language.

Learning

Structure first, story second

The ornament only worked once each letter’s skeleton was solid — Cheriyal’s bold outlines did that job.

Learning

One vocabulary, many scripts

Telugu, Devanagari and Latin each needed their own skeleton, but the faces and motifs could stay constant.
